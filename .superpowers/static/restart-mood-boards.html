The travel-diary feature (independent of which option you pick)

Whichever direction wins, the photo galleries get built on top. Three things to decide there:

Question 1 — Where do photos come from?
A. You drop them into src/content/places/<slug>/photos/ as .jpg/.webp. Astro auto-optimizes at build, lazy-loads on scroll, you commit them with the entry. Recommended.
B. Cloudinary / Imgix / similar CDN — you upload there, paste URLs. Higher cost but offloads bandwidth. Overkill for a personal site.
C. Photo extraction from Apple Photos / Google Photos (would need an export script). Most automated but adds complexity.
Question 2 — AI-generated paragraphs · what shape?
A. Per-entry frontmatter field: if paragraph: "" is empty, build script calls Claude API with the place name + your photos and writes the paragraph back. You can override anytime. Cost: ~$0.01/place. One-time at content commit. Recommended.
B. Live LLM call per page visit — slower, costs grow with traffic. Don't.
C. Manual only — you write all paragraphs. Simpler but slower for you to maintain.
D. Hybrid: Claude generates a DRAFT you edit before commit (semi-manual loop).
Question 3 — Photo layout per location
Depends on which option above you pick. Bento = grid of varied tiles. Editorial = magazine layout with text-wrap. Modern web = masonry + lightbox modal. Brutalist = simple grid, inline.

What I need from you (one specific question)

Pick one of A/B/C/D above (or two — "A bento + B editorial typography hybrid" works). OR paste a URL of a portfolio you actually love so I can reverse-engineer its DNA. OR tell me in words what's wrong with everything I've shown so far — what specifically repelled you.

If the answer is "I don't know, surprise me" — say so and I'll commit to A (Bento Studio) since it's the most photo-forward and your headline ask was photos.

Not touching the existing v3 codebase until you pick. Once locked, I'll either (a) rebuild on top of the current modular foundation (registry, layout, content collections all keep) — that's the cheap path — or (b) full rewrite if the new direction needs different bones.
